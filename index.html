    What is chronological age?

    Chronological age is the amount of time that has passed between a person's date of birth and a chosen date. It is the "real" calendar age, as opposed to an estimate of how mature someone is or how they perform on a test. In everyday life we round it to whole years, but in assessment work it is written as years;months;days because a few weeks can change which norm table is used.

    Most standardised tests, from intelligence and achievement batteries to language and motor scales, turn a raw score into a standard score by comparing the person with others of the same age. Norm tables are often split into bands of three or four months for children, and sometimes into single months for infants. If the chronological age is wrong by even a few days near the edge of a band, the wrong table can be used and the final score shifts. That is why a dependable chronological age calculator is part of every examiner's kit.

    How to calculate chronological age by hand

    The method is a simple subtraction with "borrowing", laid out in three columns: year, month and day. The date of testing goes on top, the date of birth underneath.

    Example 1 – borrowing days

    Date of testing: 5 November 2026. Date of birth: 27 March 2018.

    1. Days: 5 is smaller than 27, so borrow one month. October has 31 days: 5 + 31 = 36, and 36 − 27 = 9.
    2. Months: after borrowing, the test month is 10. 10 − 3 = 7.
    3. Years: 2026 − 2018 = 8.

    Chronological age = 8;7;9 (8 years, 7 months, 9 days).

    Example 2 – borrowing days and months

    Date of testing: 10 February 2026. Date of birth: 25 September 2019.

    1. Days: 10 is smaller than 25. Borrow January's 31 days: 10 + 31 = 41, 41 − 25 = 16. The test month drops from 2 to 1.
    2. Months: 1 is smaller than 9, so borrow one year: 1 + 12 = 13, 13 − 9 = 4. The test year drops from 2026 to 2025.
    3. Years: 2025 − 2019 = 6.

    Chronological age = 6;4;16. Try both examples in the calculator above and compare the working.

    Calendar borrowing vs 30-day borrowing

    When the day column needs a borrow, there are two common conventions:

    • Calendar method. You add the real number of days in the month before the test month (28, 29, 30 or 31). This matches the actual time that has passed and is what most online tools use.
    • 30-day method. You always add 30, whatever the month. Many paper record forms and older manuals teach this because it is quick and consistent between examiners.

    The years and months are almost always identical. Only the days can change, usually by one or two. In Example 1 the calendar method gives 8;7;9 and the 30-day method gives 8;7;8. If your test publisher's scoring software uses one rule, use the same rule by hand, so your record form and the software agree.

    Should you round chronological age?

    This is the question examiners ask most often, and the honest answer is: follow the manual of the test you are scoring. Many current manuals tell you not to round days at all. You simply use the years and months and look up the band that contains them. Other tools, especially some older developmental checklists, ask you to round 15 or 16 days or more up to the next month.

    Rounding matters most at the edge of a norm band. A child who is 7;11;20 sits in the 7;8–7;11 band if you do not round, but moves to 8;0 if you round 16+ days up. Pick the rule in the calculator's rounding menu. The result box tells you when rounding has changed the age, so nothing happens without you seeing it.

    Where chronological age is used

    Psychological testing

    Intelligence, memory and attention tests turn raw scores into standard scores using age-based norms.

    Speech & language

    Speech-language pathologists compare vocabulary, articulation and language scores with children of the same age.

    Schools & special education

    Reading and maths assessments, eligibility reports and IEP paperwork all record the student's exact age at testing.

    Paediatrics & growth

    Growth charts, milestone checklists and vaccination schedules are all based on a child's age in months.

    Occupational therapy

    Motor and sensory assessments use age bands to decide whether skills are on track.

    Sports & competitions

    Age groups are often fixed by age on a cut-off date, which is a chronological age question too.

    Common mistakes when calculating chronological age

    • Putting the dates the wrong way round. The date of testing always goes on top, the date of birth underneath.
    • Forgetting to lower the month after borrowing days. When you borrow for the day column, the month column on the top line goes down by one.
    • Using the wrong month to borrow from. With the calendar method, borrow the days of the month before the test month, not the birth month.
    • Mixing US and European date formats. 04/05/2019 is 5 April in the US and 4 May in Europe. Check the format before you enter it.
    • Rounding when the manual says not to. Rounding can move a child into the next norm band and change the standard score.
    • Using the date the report was written. Chronological age is the age on the day the test was given. If testing ran over several days, use the first day unless your manual says otherwise.

    Chronological age in months

    Infant and toddler scales, and growth charts, often use total months instead of years and months. Multiply the years by 12 and add the months. A child who is 2;7;14 is 31 months and 14 days old. Here are the whole years for quick reference:

    FAQ

    Chronological age questions

    What is chronological age?

    Chronological age is the exact amount of time a person has been alive, measured from the date of birth to a given date and written in years, months and days.

    How do you write chronological age?

    It is usually written as years;months;days, for example 8;7;9 means 8 years, 7 months and 9 days. Some forms use hyphens (8-7-9) or periods (8.7.9) instead of semicolons.

    Should I round chronological age up?

    Only if your test manual tells you to. Many current test manuals say not to round days. Some older tools round 15 or 16 days up to the next month. This calculator lets you pick the rule your manual uses.

    Why do two calculators give a different number of days?

    Some borrow the real number of days in the previous month (28 to 31) and some borrow a fixed 30 days, as many paper record forms do. The years and months stay the same, but the days can differ by one or two.

    Is chronological age the same as corrected age?

    No. Corrected age subtracts the weeks a baby was born early. Chronological age is always counted from the actual date of birth.

    What is the difference between chronological age and mental age?

    Chronological age is calendar time since birth. Mental age or age equivalent is an estimate of the age level a person performs at on a test. The two are compared, but they measure different things.

    Can I calculate the age of a whole class?

    Yes. Paste a list of names and birth dates into the class list tool, choose one test date, and you get every student's age with a CSV you can copy into a spreadsheet.

    Is my data saved anywhere?

    No. All calculations run in your browser. Names and dates are not sent or stored.
